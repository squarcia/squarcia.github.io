Selected work

Teaching a quadruped to follow instructions it was never explicitly rewarded for

Imperial College London · SPIKE Group, led by Prof. Alessandra Russo

Standard reinforcement learning assumes the next best action depends only on the current state. Many useful robot tasks break that assumption: visit A, then B, but never enter C. I developed a neuro-symbolic reinforcement learning algorithm that encodes this kind of temporally extended, non-Markovian task as a symbolic structure the learning agent can reason over, and trained a Unitree Go2 Pro quadruped against it.

  • Built the full pipeline in the NVIDIA Isaac Simulator: environment modelling, reward specification, training and policy evaluation.
  • Ran sim-to-real transfer onto the physical robot, with quantitative validation of the gap between simulated and measured performance.
  • Extended the approach explored in my MSc thesis, Reward Machines for Real World Navigation.

Part of an international collaboration with the University of Cardiff, the University of Brescia and UCLA, funded by the DEVCOM Army Research Laboratory.
